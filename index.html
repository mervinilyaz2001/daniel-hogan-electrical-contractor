Why it's worth sorting

Electrical problems don't stay small.

Most faults give you a warning first. This is what those warnings usually mean.

  1. 01

    Tripping is a warning, not a nuisance

    An RCD trips because it has picked up current going where it shouldn't. Resetting it again and again doesn't fix that. It just ignores it.

  2. 02

    Heat means a loose connection

    A warm socket, a buzzing switch or a brown mark on the faceplate all point to a connection that's breaking down. That's how electrical fires start.

  3. 03

    Old wiring wasn't built for today

    A house wired decades ago wasn't planned for electric showers, tumble dryers, heat pumps or an EV. Extension leads and adaptors take up the slack, and that's where overloads happen.

A planned upgrade happens on a day that suits you. A fault happens whenever it likes.

Job sheet Something's leaking to earth

Trip switch keeps going

Most likely cause
An RCD trips when it senses current leaking to earth. The usual culprits are a faulty appliance (kettles, immersions, washing machines), water in an outside light or socket, or a fault on one circuit. If it's a single breaker tripping instead, that circuit is overloaded or has a short.
What we'd do
Test the circuits and appliances to find the fault, repair it, and test the RCD. If the board is old, we'll talk you through an upgrade so one fault doesn't knock out the whole house.
Safe to check first
Unplug everything on that circuit, reset the switch, then plug things back in one at a time. If it trips when one appliance goes in, that's likely your culprit. If it trips with nothing plugged in, leave it off and ring us.

Typical jobs

The kind of jobs we take on

Everyday problems, and how they usually get sorted. Tap a photo to see it bigger, or the label to read about the service.

Photos are for illustration.

  • Fuse board upgrade

    Goodbye, pull-out fuses

    Ceramic fuses, no RCD, and a fuse that blows every time the kettle and the immersion are on together. Sound familiar? The old board comes out, a modern consumer unit with RCD protection goes in, and every circuit is tested and labelled, so you finally know which switch does what.

  • EV charger

    Plugged in at home

    A new electric car, and a three-pin plug that takes all night and then some. A proper charger on its own circuit, the cable run neatly from the fuse board, and it's set to charge overnight, so the car is ready every morning.

  • Shop lighting

    A shop floor that sells

    Old fluorescent tubes flicker over the shelves and make everything look tired. Track spots and LED lighting put the light where the stock is and use less electricity, and the work is planned to keep disruption to the business down.

  • House rewire

    A rewire, before the plaster goes on

    First fix is the messy part: walls chased, back boxes set in, and new cable run to every socket and light. It's also the cheapest time to change your mind, so this is when to say "could we have a socket here instead?"

  • Kitchen lighting

    A kitchen you can actually see in

    One pendant in the middle of the ceiling, and a shadow over the worktop wherever you stand. LED downlights on a dimmer, strip lights under the presses and a switch by every door turn it into the brightest room in the house.

  • TV and cabling

    The TV, minus the cables

    A big new TV on the wall, and four cables dangling down to the floor. Power and aerial points go in behind the screen, the cables disappear into the wall, and all that's left is the picture.

  • Outdoor lighting

    A light on when you get home

    Fumbling for your keys in the dark on a winter's evening is nobody's idea of a welcome. Sensor lights at the door and along the drive, plus a weatherproof outdoor socket for the power washer, all on a protected circuit.

  • Workshop power

    Power to the workbench

    A new machine arrives, and the workshop has two sockets and a flickering strip light. Proper power to every bench, a supply for the machine (three-phase where it's needed) and bright LED lighting you can actually work under.

  • Smart switches

    Smart lights that still work from the wall

    Smart bulbs are great until someone flicks the switch off and the app goes dead. Smart switches fix that: the lights work from the wall, your phone or a timer. Many need a neutral wire at the switch, so we check yours before you buy.
